# Phase 1 — Strategy Brief

A logo is a compression algorithm for a brand. You cannot compress what you have
not defined. Work through these three blocks with the user before drawing.

Ask in batches, not one at a time. If the user gives thin answers, propose a
sharp version and let them correct it — people recognise the right answer faster
than they generate it.

---

## Block 1 — Brand identity

**What does it stand for?**

- What does the business actually do, in one plain sentence a stranger would understand?
- What are its core values and its mission?
- What is its personality? Give it three adjectives. If two of them are synonyms, replace one.
- What single emotion should the mark evoke on first sight?

The emotion is the most important line in the brief. Force a choice of exactly
one primary:

| Emotion | Reads as | Typical shape/colour pull |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Trust | Stable, institutional, safe | Squares, horizontals, deep blues, symmetry |
| Innovation | Forward, sharp, technical | Diagonals, triangles, electric accents, asymmetry |
| Playfulness | Warm, approachable, human | Circles, bounce, saturated brights, rounded joins |
| Luxury | Restrained, rare, considered | Thin weights, wide spacing, monochrome + one metal |
| Craft | Made, honest, tactile | Organic curves, ink traps, warm neutrals, texture |
| Speed | Urgent, efficient, kinetic | Italics, forward lean, sharp exits, high contrast |
| Care | Soft, attentive, protective | Enclosing forms, arcs, greens/blushes, low contrast |

A secondary emotion is permitted. A tertiary is not — three emotions produce a
mark that evokes none of them.

## Block 2 — Target audience

**Who is it for?**

- Primary audience: demographics, but more usefully — what do they value, and what are they afraid of?
- Where will they encounter this mark first? (App store icon? Shop front? Invoice? Conference badge?) This decides which deliverable is the hero.
- What visual language does this audience already trust? What reads as cheap to them specifically?

The last question matters more than the first. "Cheap" is audience-relative: a
gradient reads as dated to a design buyer and as modern to a mass consumer.

## Block 3 — Competitive differentiation

**Why this one and not the others?**

- What genuinely makes this brand different — not the marketing claim, the actual difference?
- Who are the three closest competitors, and what do their marks look like?
- Which industry conventions must be honoured (so the brand is legible as belonging to its category), and which must be broken (so it is not invisible)?

Both halves of that last question are required. A mark that honours every
convention is wallpaper; one that breaks every convention is a puzzle.

---

## Output format

End Phase 1 by writing this block and showing it to the user verbatim:

```
BRAND BRIEF — <name>

Sentence:  <one sentence a stranger would understand>
Emotion:   <one word, from the table>
Audience:  <one line>

Differentiator: <the real one>
Honour:         <convention to keep>
Break:          <convention to reject>

Hero deliverable: <where it is seen first>
Assumptions:      <anything you filled in for the user — flag every one>
```

Do not proceed until the user has seen this. If they correct a line, rewrite the
whole block rather than patching it — the lines interact.
